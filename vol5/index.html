Volume V — The Physics Interface

Where the mathematics meets measurement: what can be anchored, what can be measured, and what cannot.

What this volume is. Two books about the boundary between proved mathematics and physical measurement. Book 23 sets the anchors: the C-1 chirality import needs a scale anchor — the 21 cm line — and an orientation anchor — the imaginary-negative-volume sector occupancy — each with its declared scope. Book 24 runs the measurement campaign: the IO-word sweep that corrected the mass law, built the dial as a working instrument, and proved, one by one, the things the 1820 cannot select.

Why they belong together. An anchor says "this is where the theory touches the world." A campaign says "this is what the touching can and cannot establish." Book 23 declares the contact points; Book 24 stress-tests them with 189 ledger claims, full derivations in Appendix P, and a run-61 correction that withdrew a published-worthy claim rather than defend it.

Dependencies. Book 23 depends on the C-1 chirality import contract and measured atomic data (declared per section). Book 24 depends on the Volume I audit's 1820 machinery — the Casimir construction, the C8 candidate space, the epsilon operator — and nothing else. Neither book promotes a result past its scope.

The shared discipline

Every contact point between the mathematics and physics in this volume carries a scope label, and the label is the content:

PROVED — the mathematics establishes it (the corrected mass law, the dial inequality, the no-go theorems). CHECKED — numerically verified to stated precision with tripwires disclosed. IMPORT — brought in from measured physics or Kit's directive, never derived (the 21 cm parameters, the sector occupancy record). ASSERTED / CONDITIONAL / INCOMPLETE — exactly what the words say. FALSIFIED — withdrawn in the open, with the correction recorded (the m₂⁻⁴ law, the scalar pair, the uniform bridges).

Proved negatives are first-class results here. "No IO-word measurement can select m₀/m₂" and "nothing selects inside the 12-dimensional candidate space" are theorems, not disappointments — they close doors the theory must stop knocking on.
