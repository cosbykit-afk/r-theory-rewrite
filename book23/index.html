§1 — The C-1 import contract and what an anchor is

Contract reference: C-1 (chirality law) in vol4/physics_interface/IMPORT_CONTRACTS_DRAFT.md. An anchor is a declared empirical calibration point for an import. It fixes, in writing, which measured numbers the import's bridge equations are allowed to use — and, just as importantly, which questions the import does not answer. Anchors do not derive the import; they discipline it.

This book publishes two anchors for C-1:

  • CHIRAL-ANCHOR-01 — the 21 cm neutral-hydrogen hyperfine line: the scale anchor.
  • CHIRAL-ANCHOR-02 — the imaginary-negative-volume sector occupancy: the orientation anchor.

§2 — Anchor 01: the 21 cm line IMPORT

Principle. The C-1 physics-interface import is calibrated against the measured 21 cm hyperfine line of neutral hydrogen. The line anchors scale (frequency, wavelength, time) as dimensionless ratios against explicitly declared references; orientation content is carried separately by the sector assignment (Anchor 02).

History and transition

  • Predicted 1944 — H. van de Hulst.
  • Observed 1951 — Ewen & Purcell; Muller & Oort; Christiansen et al.
  • Species: neutral atomic hydrogen (H I).
  • Transition: ground-state (1s) hyperfine spin-flip, F=1 → F=0 (electron spin reversed relative to proton spin).
  • Radiation type: magnetic dipole (highly forbidden) — mean lifetime on the order of eleven million years.

Chirality readout

In a magnetic field the line splits into Zeeman components; the σ+ and σ− components are circularly polarized with opposite handedness. The assigned handedness of each σ component reverses with the sign of the line-of-sight field Blos. The observable is the handedness of the Zeeman σ components — this is the chirality-sensitive observable built on the anchor. Scope note: the bare line frequency itself carries no orientation content; it is a scalar standard.

Kit's directive (2026-09-28): all numerical values in the calibration program are driven off this anchor. The anchor values themselves are empirical (measured); the root status — that every calibration number chains back to ν21 — is Kit's asserted directive, not a derived result.
